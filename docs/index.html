01 System Overview

Project Goal

Build an autonomous driving agent capable of consistently hot-lapping within the top 95% of human lap times at Monza in the BMW Z4 GT3. The agent observes raw simulator telemetry and outputs control signals injected into the game via a virtual gamepad at 30 Hz, with no vision, no map, just physics state.

Beyond raw performance, the model could serve as a vehicle setup testing tool where you change suspension settings and measure the lap time response, or as a reference overlay showing human drivers where time is left on track.

Model Inputs 54 signals — evolved across runs

The observation space was refined throughout the project. Early runs used a smaller feature set; the full list below reflects the final configuration. All signals are read from Assetto Corsa's shared memory at 30 Hz and normalized before being passed to the network.

Driver context (fed back) Gas · Brake · Gear · RPMs · Steering angle
Vehicle dynamics Speed · Velocity (x/y/z) · G-Force (x/y/z) · Angular velocity (x/y/z)
Orientation & ride Heading · Pitch · Roll · CG height · Ride height (front/rear)
Per-wheel × 4 (FL / FR / RL / RR) — 28 values Slip ratio · Load · Pressure · Angular speed · Core temperature · Camber · Suspension travel
Track position Normalized position along track · Coordinates (x/y/z) · Tyres out of bounds

Model Outputs

  • Steering−360° → +360°
  • Throttle0 → 1
  • Brake0 → 1
  • Gear Shiftup / neutral / down (manual runs)

Algorithm

  • BaseSAC (Soft Actor-Critic)
  • CorrectionDisCor
  • DemosSAC-fD / behavior cloning (tested)
  • CurriculumRacing line adherence → lap time

The curriculum starts with the racing line reward at full weight, rewarding the model for staying close to the optimal racing line. This gets the model moving around the track without needing to worry about pace. Over the first ~100k steps the weight decays to near zero, shifting the primary signal to a speed-based lap time reward. The idea is to first establish track navigation, then optimize for outright pace.

Infrastructure

  • SimulatorAssetto Corsa (Windows)
  • Physics rate30 Hz shared memory
  • Input methodViGEmBus virtual Xbox 360
  • FrameworkPyTorch + CUDA 12.6

02 Model Training Log

Every training run in chronological order. All runs are on Monza with the BMW Z4 GT3. ACGym reference lap: 1:51:64. Our best valid lap: 1:54.127 after extending the Apr 26 model.

Why so few valid laps? Monza's turn 1 is an extremely heavy braking zone immediately after the start/finish straight. Most models learned to navigate the rest of the circuit but could not modulate braking sharply enough to scrub speed in time. The car would go off-track at turn 1 on every single lap, triggering a reset, which made it impossible to post a clean lap time. A "valid" lap here means the car completed the full circuit without any resets.

03 Key Findings

What we learned across three months and 14+ training runs.

What Worked

  • Sync inference to physics packets The single most impactful change in the project. Blocking model inference until each 30 Hz physics packet arrived, rather than running inference on a 30 Hz timer, was the difference between a non-functional and a functional training loop. Before this fix, inputs were at the right frequency but misaligned to the simulator's update window.
  • ABS and TC on during early training Factory defaults (TC 3, ABS 5) produced significantly cleaner control outputs. The model learned to hold full throttle on straights and trail brake cleanly at corner entry. Without driver aids, front tires locked under braking and the model learned to avoid braking altogether rather than modulate it.
  • Auto-shifting before manual shifting Building solid driving fundamentals under auto-shifting for ~1.5 M steps before introducing manual gear selection produced our best result, eventually reaching 1:54.127 after continued training. Every run that introduced manual shifting before the model could drive consistently caused the driving behavior to collapse entirely.
  • Separate shifter and driver models Decoupling the gear-selection network from the driving control network showed promise and is the most promising direction for getting manual shifting to work without degrading driving behavior.
  • Curriculum on racing line adherence Gradually reducing the reward weight on staying near the racing line kept early training stable and directed exploration. A reset penalty in the curriculum helped discourage the model from exploiting short track segments.

What Didn't Work

  • Linux server training ViGEmBus is Windows-only with no Linux equivalent. Assetto Corsa's shared memory interface also differs between OSes. Additionally, training speed gains from the server were marginal since the AC physics simulation runs at real-time and cannot be accelerated without full parallelization across multiple game instances.
  • Behavior cloning pretraining Multiple BC approaches were tried, including 10-epoch pretraining, vanilla SAC updates on a demo buffer, and 2 to 5 epoch BC. All produced erratic steering or a frozen car in the subsequent RL phase. The BC signal didn't transfer well to online learning. One run was also corrupted by a bug in the shift-up/shift-down encoding in the demo data.
  • Training without ABS from the start Without ABS, the model consistently learned to avoid heavy braking to prevent tire lockup rather than learning to modulate braking pressure. The result was a model that couldn't slow down for tight corners and went off-track at every significant braking zone.
  • Reward exploitation In the Apr 20 long run, the model discovered it could maximize the speed-based reward by dive-bombing two corners. The high-speed reward outweighed the off-track penalty enough that the behavior was stable. Proper reward shaping to close this loophole is still an open problem.
  • Manual shifting introduced cold The expanded action space (gear shifts on top of steering/throttle/brake) was too large to explore effectively without a strong driving prior already in place. Every attempt to train manual shifting before the driving fundamentals were solid caused the policy to collapse.

05 Results Overview

How the final model compared against the original target.

Human world record 1:46.17

The benchmark target was to build an agent that could consistently lap Monza within 95% of the human world-record pace.

5% slower target 1:51.479

A 1:46.17 lap is 106.17 seconds. Multiplying by 1.05 gives a target cutoff of 111.4785 seconds, or 1:51.479.

Best valid model lap 1:54.127

The model improved substantially, but it was 2.648 seconds slower than the 95% target, so it did not meet the original goal.

Theoretical optimal 1:52.188

Assetto Corsa also reports a theoretical optimal lap by splicing together the fastest sectors across multiple laps. The model's theoretical best was 0.710 seconds off the target.

06 Project Timeline

Condensed from weekly meeting notes and progress reports.

Show full timeline
Jan 16, 2026

Kickoff

Researched existing projects (ACGym, ACRL, NVIDIA presentations). Decided on Monza and the BMW Z4 GT3 as the first test configuration. Used ACTI as a first-pass telemetry tool. The team divided up initial work: one member began building a proprietary data collection pipeline while the other researched imitation learning approaches.

Week of Jan 19

Data Pipeline + Input Method

Built a custom telemetry collection application. Recorded 15 laps of Monza at 30 Hz in CSV format, including steering, tire state, actuators, and more. There is no direct steering injection API in Assetto Corsa, so we settled on ViGEmBus, a Windows driver that emulates a physical Xbox 360 controller with extremely low latency.

Jan 23, 2026

First Advisor Meeting

Presented the data pipeline. Confirmed the virtual gamepad approach was the right call. Next milestone: replay recorded telemetry through the virtual controller and compare resulting telemetry to the original to validate the round-trip.

Weeks 4–5

ACGym Baseline Replication

Successfully replicated ACGym's training results end-to-end as a sanity check and performance baseline. Learned how to run Assetto Corsa in "headless" mode (reduced resolution, minimized window). Key observation: the automatic gearbox loses a significant amount of lap time, and manual shifting is required to compete with human lap times.

Week 7

Porting to Custom Environment

Goal: move the SAC training loop out of ACGym and into our own codebase. Began designing manual gear-shifting as an additional model output. Started pursuing server access for longer overnight training runs.

Mar 20

Linux Server: Dead End

Custom environment running, but after 8 hours the model sat in place and braked. Root cause unknown (curriculum was the main suspect). Linux server training hit multiple hard walls:

  • ViGEmBus is Windows-only and no equivalent driver exists for Linux.
  • Assetto Corsa's shared memory interface works differently on Linux.
  • Training speed gains were also limited since the AC physics clock runs at real-time and can't be accelerated without full parallelization.

Decision: establish a working baseline on Windows first.

Mar 27

Critical Breakthrough: Physics Packet Sync

Built a distilled version of the AC control loop. Identified the core issue blocking progress: model inference must be explicitly synced to each physics packet arrival. Previously we ran inference at 30 Hz on a timer, which was the same frequency as physics but not aligned to it. After blocking on packet arrival before each inference call, the model matched or exceeded ACGym performance.

Second discovery: training with ABS and TC enabled (factory defaults: TC 3, ABS 5) produces dramatically cleaner outputs, including full throttle on straights and clean trail braking at corner entry.

Apr 10

First Batch of Simultaneous Training Runs

Ran three different configurations overnight in parallel. Empirically confirmed the ABS/TC finding. One run showed the model beginning to learn gear shifts, which was not yet optimal but demonstrated the behavior was learnable.

Apr 15–24

Demonstration Pretraining Sprint

Multiple runs exploring behavior cloning and demo-guided SAC. Investigated offline/online buffer ratios. Manual shifting consistently caused trained behavior to collapse. Decoupling a dedicated shifter model from the driving model began to look like the right path forward.

Apr 26

Best Result: 1:54.127 Valid Lap

Trained with auto-shifting for ~1.5 million steps to build driving fundamentals, then transitioned to manual. The auto-shift phase produced the cleanest driving behavior observed. Continuing that same model for another 8 hours produced a valid lap of 1:54.127, with Assetto Corsa reporting a theoretical optimal of 1:52.188. The manual shift transition caused immediate collapse.

Apr 28 (Current)

Latest Run

Demo-assisted run with an automated auto-shifting → manual-shifting curriculum and decayed offline sampling. The model developed a strong brake preference and the car would not drive. Investigation continues.
